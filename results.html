The supporting measurements.

How it was measured.

The methods in full
  • Coating. Swatches dipped in the bath for 60 s, padded by hand with a weighted roller between lint free paper, and cured at 150°C for 3 minutes. Cotton was scoured first; the Finisterre fabric was the same cloth as the commercial finish, supplied without a finish.
  • Samples. Three swatches per condition, all cut from one coated batch, so the spread shows swatch to swatch variation, not batch to batch. The durability series measured the same 1.5 cm swatches again at every stage; separate spare swatches went to the microscope.
  • Contact angle. Ossila contact angle goniometer. Each drop was filmed at 30 frames per second and fitted frame by frame by the software; frames with a poor fit were rejected. Each swatch's value is the median angle 2 to 10 s after the drop lands, and the reported value is the mean of three swatches.
  • Roll off angle. Tilting stage read from an angle gauge, up to 90°. The first test used a smaller drop; the second test and the whole durability series used a drop of about 33 µL. A drop that had not moved by 90° is recorded as pinned, marked on the charts, and left out of that average, which makes the affected average look better than it was.
  • Wash cycle. 10 minutes in 500 mL of deionised water with 2.5 g of non ionic detergent (Alcojet) at 40°C, on a magnetic stirrer at 300 rpm, whole sample set together, then rinsed and air dried overnight with no heat. A lab screening wash, not a domestic wash or ISO 6330.
  • Abrasion pass. One 10 cm stroke, about 3 s, of a 1.5 cm swatch on a microscope slide under 157 g (about 6.8 kPa), drawn by hand across 1000 grit sandpaper. A lab screening method, not Martindale.
  • Microscopy and composition. Hitachi TM4000Plus SEM at 15 kV after 10 nm of gold. EDX from single area scans without standards. ATR-FTIR on a Shimadzu IRSpirit with a QATR-S accessory, three spectra per sample. Benchtop XRF on a Fischerscope XAN 250.
  • Statistics. The charts show every swatch, with lines and rules at the mean. With one batch and three swatches, the trends are described rather than claimed as significant; the dissertation reports the tests and corrections.
  • Data. Every swatch value behind the charts is in observations.csv, with column notes in the data README. It is built straight from the lab exports, not read off the figures.

What I would do next.

  • Fix the fresh shedding. Rinse the coated fabric and repeat at a controlled coating weight, to see what is pinning the drop.
  • Use standard tests. Spray rating, a rain test, and washing to a standard method.
  • Measure what holds the drop. Advancing and receding angles at one drop volume.
  • Separate drying from damage. Some finishes recover with heat, and these swatches were only air dried.
  • Repeat across batches. A second coated batch, with repeat scans on the worn swatches.
