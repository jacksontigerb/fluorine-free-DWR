In the lab · Final results

The results.

Repellency, shedding, 20 wash cycles and 200 abrasion passes, and the worn surfaces under the electron microscope. Here is what the measurements say, including the parts that did not go the way I hoped.

Testing finished July 2026 · page checked against the lab data 13 September 2026 · full analysis in the dissertation (PDF, 17 MB)

Abstract

I made a fluorine free water repellent emulsion (stearyl acrylate with a little silicone and a blocked isocyanate crosslinker), with and without titanium dioxide particles, and applied it by dip, pad and cure to scoured cotton and to Finisterre's fabric supplied without a finish. The benchmark is Finisterre's own commercial PFAS free finish on that same fabric.

  1. Beading. On cotton my coatings reached 132 to 135° contact angle, similar to Finisterre's finish on its own fabric (132°). On Finisterre's fabric my emulsion reached 108°.
  2. Shedding. Fresh, only the commercial finish released a smaller drop. With a larger drop (about 33 µL) everything released, but on the same fabric the commercial finish was far ahead: 24° against 59°.
  3. Washing. Over 20 wash cycles the commercial finish went from 24° to 78° roll off, and one of its three swatches stopped releasing the drop, while its contact angle moved by 3°. My emulsion on the same fabric ended in the same place, around 80°.

Limits: three swatches per condition from one coated batch, and lab screening methods for washing and abrasion rather than textile standards. Read the trends as consistent, not as firm statistics. How it was measured · read the dissertation (PDF, 17 MB).

The samples

Seven samples, two fabrics.

Both fabrics, plain cotton and Finisterre's own, have an uncoated control and a swatch of my emulsion on its own. On cotton I also ran two particle treatments at the same loading. Finisterre's commercial finish sits on Finisterre's fabric, so the fair comparison with it is my emulsion on that same fabric, F-A1.

  • C-U / F-U. Uncoated cotton and uncoated Finisterre fabric, the controls.
  • A1. My emulsion only, no particles.
  • B3. Emulsion plus 3 wt% bare anatase titanium dioxide.
  • C3. Emulsion plus 3 wt% anatase treated with a silane (VTMS).
  • F-DWR. Finisterre's existing commercial finish, the benchmark.

Repellency

Every coating makes water bead.

Water contact angle is the first test: how round a drop sits on the surface. Above 90° counts as hydrophobic. (Superhydrophobic usually means above 150° with drops that roll at a few degrees; none of these fabrics are that, the commercial finish included.) Every coating clears the hydrophobic line, and on cotton mine sit at about the same angle as the commercial finish does on its own fabric.

  • One limit of this test. Static contact angle does not separate the two particle treatments, bare against silane treated. If the silane helps, it should show up in shedding and durability, not here.
  • A corrected control. An early reading put uncoated cotton near 110°, but that drop was resting on loose fibres across the weave rather than on the fabric. Measured again, uncoated cotton wets out at about 39° (one swatch), so the coated fabrics sit roughly 95° above their own baseline.

Shedding

Beading is not the same as shedding.

A high contact angle means a drop sits as a bead. Roll off angle asks the harder question: tilt the surface, and at what angle does the drop actually move? Both tests here were done before any washing, and on this one the commercial finish is well ahead of mine.

Mean roll off angle before washing, larger drop (about 33 µL), 3 swatches each. Lower is better.

The difference is in how the drop lets go: on my coatings the back edge of the drop grips the fabric instead of releasing. What wear does to that gap is the next section.

Why contact angle is not enough

Durability

Abrasion and washing tell opposite stories.

A coating is only useful if it lasts. I put the whole sample set through 200 abrasion passes and 20 wash cycles, measuring roll off and contact angle on the same swatches at every stage, then put spare worn swatches under the electron microscope. Both wear tests are lab screening methods rather than textile standards; the methods say exactly what a pass and a cycle were.

Where that leaves it. Washing is the shared weakness for shedding, and it hit the commercial finish hardest, taking it from well ahead of my emulsion to level with it on the same fabric. Contact angle barely registered that, which is why roll off is the number to watch. Coating material was still detectable after both tests. With three swatches from one batch these are consistent trends rather than firm statistics.

Composition

What is actually on the fabric.

SEM-EDX reads the elements on the fibre surface. Titanium tracks the titanium dioxide I added, and silicon tracks the silicone and silane in the coating. Both show up where they should, and the commercial finish gives a different elemental picture to mine.

Chemistry

What the infrared shows.

Infrared spectroscopy checks the chemistry rather than the elements. On the fabric it confirms the coating chemistry is on the fibre, with siloxane bands from the silicone. On the titanium dioxide powder it is where the silane treatment shows up.

Digging deeper

The measurements behind the headline results.

These back up the main findings: an independent check on the titanium signal, the particle and coating texture under the microscope, and the smaller measurements behind the method choices.

Methods

How it was measured.

The short version of the methods, so the numbers above can be read properly. The full detail, reagents and quantities are in the dissertation.

  • CoatingSwatches dipped in the bath for 60 s, padded by hand with a weighted roller between lint free paper, and cured at 150°C for 3 minutes. Cotton was scoured first; the Finisterre fabric was the same cloth as the commercial finish, supplied without a finish.
  • SamplesThree swatches per condition, all cut from one coated batch, so the spread shows swatch to swatch variation, not batch to batch. The durability series measured the same 1.5 cm swatches again at every stage; separate spare swatches went to the microscope.
  • Contact angleOssila contact angle goniometer. Each drop was filmed at 30 frames per second and fitted frame by frame by the software; frames with a poor fit were rejected. Each swatch's value is the median angle 2 to 10 s after the drop lands, and the reported value is the mean of three swatches.
  • Roll off angleTilting stage read from an angle gauge, up to 90°. The first test used a smaller drop; the second test and the whole durability series used a drop of about 33 µL. A drop that had not moved by 90° is recorded as pinned, marked on the charts, and left out of that average, which makes the affected average look better than it was.
  • Wash cycle10 minutes in 500 mL of deionised water with 2.5 g of non ionic detergent (Alcojet) at 40°C, on a magnetic stirrer at 300 rpm, whole sample set together, then rinsed and air dried overnight with no heat. A lab screening wash, not a domestic wash or ISO 6330.
  • Abrasion passOne 10 cm stroke, about 3 s, of a 1.5 cm swatch on a microscope slide under 157 g (about 6.8 kPa), drawn by hand across 1000 grit sandpaper. A lab screening method, not Martindale.
  • Microscopy and compositionHitachi TM4000Plus SEM at 15 kV after 10 nm of gold. EDX from single area scans without standards. ATR-FTIR on a Shimadzu IRSpirit with a QATR-S accessory, three spectra per sample. Benchtop XRF on a Fischerscope XAN 250.
  • StatisticsThe charts show every swatch, with lines and rules at the mean. With one batch and three swatches, the trends are described rather than claimed as significant; the dissertation reports the tests and corrections.
  • DataEvery swatch value behind the charts is in observations.csv, with column notes in the data README. It is built straight from the lab exports, not read off the figures.

In summary

The short version.

On cotton my coatings bead water as well as Finisterre's finish does on its own fabric. Fresh, they do not shed as well: on the same fabric the commercial finish was far ahead. Washing took that lead away, and it did so while contact angle barely moved. The clearest lesson is about measurement: contact angle, the number most often reported, missed a change in shedding that roll off picked up easily.

The lab work is finished, and the full writeup, with the statistics and caveats, is in my dissertation (PDF, 17 MB). If the work were taken further, these would settle the open questions:

  • Fix the fresh shedding. Make the emulsion again so a fresh drop releases as easily as it beads. A rinse test and a repeat at controlled coating weight would show whether leftover surfactant or an over thick film is doing the pinning.
  • Use standard tests. A spray rating (ISO 4920), washing to a standard method with a defined drying step, and a fixed drop volume for roll off would put the shedding numbers on terms other labs can compare.
  • Repeat across batches. A second coated batch, and repeat EDX and infrared scans on worn swatches, would show whether the silane treated particles really do hold on better.

A note on these numbers. These are the final measured values. Three swatches per condition, cut from one coated batch, tested with lab screening methods, so the trends are read cautiously.
