Seven samples, two fabrics.

Finisterre's commercial finish sits on Finisterre's fabric, so the fair comparison with it is my emulsion on that same fabric, F-A1.

Every coating makes water bead.

Water contact angle is how round a drop sits on the surface. Above 90° counts as hydrophobic, and every coating clears that line.

Beading is not the same as shedding.

Roll off angle asks the harder question: tilt the surface, and at what angle does the drop actually move? Both tests here were done before any washing, and on this one the commercial finish is well ahead of mine.

Mean roll off angle before washing, larger drop (about 33 µL), 3 swatches each. Lower is better.

Abrasion and washing tell opposite stories.

I put the whole sample set through 200 abrasion passes and 20 wash cycles, measuring roll off and contact angle on the same swatches at every stage. Both wear tests are lab screening methods rather than textile standards.

What is actually on the fabric.

SEM-EDX reads the elements on the fibre surface. Titanium tracks the titanium dioxide I added, and silicon tracks the silicone and silane in the coating.

What the infrared shows.

Infrared spectroscopy checks the chemistry rather than the elements. On the titanium dioxide powder it is where the silane treatment shows up.

The measurements behind the headline results.

How it was measured.

The methods in full
  • Coating. Swatches dipped in the bath for 60 s, padded by hand with a weighted roller between lint free paper, and cured at 150°C for 3 minutes. Cotton was scoured first; the Finisterre fabric was the same cloth as the commercial finish, supplied without a finish.
  • Samples. Three swatches per condition, all cut from one coated batch, so the spread shows swatch to swatch variation, not batch to batch. The durability series measured the same 1.5 cm swatches again at every stage; separate spare swatches went to the microscope.
  • Contact angle. Ossila contact angle goniometer. Each drop was filmed at 30 frames per second and fitted frame by frame by the software; frames with a poor fit were rejected. Each swatch's value is the median angle 2 to 10 s after the drop lands, and the reported value is the mean of three swatches.
  • Roll off angle. Tilting stage read from an angle gauge, up to 90°. The first test used a smaller drop; the second test and the whole durability series used a drop of about 33 µL. A drop that had not moved by 90° is recorded as pinned, marked on the charts, and left out of that average, which makes the affected average look better than it was.
  • Wash cycle. 10 minutes in 500 mL of deionised water with 2.5 g of non ionic detergent (Alcojet) at 40°C, on a magnetic stirrer at 300 rpm, whole sample set together, then rinsed and air dried overnight with no heat. A lab screening wash, not a domestic wash or ISO 6330.
  • Abrasion pass. One 10 cm stroke, about 3 s, of a 1.5 cm swatch on a microscope slide under 157 g (about 6.8 kPa), drawn by hand across 1000 grit sandpaper. A lab screening method, not Martindale.
  • Microscopy and composition. Hitachi TM4000Plus SEM at 15 kV after 10 nm of gold. EDX from single area scans without standards. ATR-FTIR on a Shimadzu IRSpirit with a QATR-S accessory, three spectra per sample. Benchtop XRF on a Fischerscope XAN 250.
  • Statistics. The charts show every swatch, with lines and rules at the mean. With one batch and three swatches, the trends are described rather than claimed as significant; the dissertation reports the tests and corrections.
  • Data. Every swatch value behind the charts is in observations.csv, with column notes in the data README. It is built straight from the lab exports, not read off the figures.

The short version.

On cotton my coatings bead water as well as Finisterre's finish does on its own fabric. Fresh, they do not shed as well: on the same fabric the commercial finish was far ahead. Washing took that lead away, and it did so while contact angle barely moved. The clearest lesson is about measurement: contact angle, the number most often reported, missed a change in shedding that roll off picked up easily.

The lab work is finished, and the full writeup is in my dissertation (PDF, 17 MB). If the work were taken further, these would settle the open questions.

  • Fix the fresh shedding. A rinse test and a repeat at controlled coating weight would show whether leftover surfactant or an over thick film is doing the pinning.
  • Use standard tests. A spray rating (ISO 4920), a rain test (Bundesmann, ISO 9865), washing to a standard method, and a fixed drop volume for roll off.
  • Measure what holds the drop. Advancing and receding angles, on a tilting plate at one drop volume, would show directly why a drop pins.
  • Separate drying from damage. Some finishes recover their repellency after heat, and these swatches were only air dried, so some of the wash loss may be recoverable.
  • Repeat across batches. A second coated batch, and repeat EDX and infrared scans on worn swatches, would show whether the silane treated particles really do hold on better.
