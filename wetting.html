Why contact angle is not enough.

Weight helps a drop break free, so a bigger drop can roll off a surface that pins a smaller one. Above about 20 µL gravity also flattens the drop, which this model ignores.

Worth trying.

  • Same angle, two explanations. Set the smooth surface angle to 108° and roughness to 2.2×. With solid contact at 100% the drop fills the texture and sits at about 133°. Drop solid contact to 48% and it rests on trapped air at about the same angle.
  • Roughness cuts both ways. Set the smooth surface angle below 90° and turn the roughness up. The contact angle falls: texture makes a surface that already wets, wet more.
  • Drop size changes the answer. From the starting settings, a 5 µL drop stays pinned at 90° while a 20 µL drop runs off at about 38°. That is why roll off numbers are only comparable at the same drop volume.

In my results, finishes with similar contact angles behaved very differently when tilted, and washing changed shedding a lot while contact angle barely moved. This page shows, on an idealised surface, why that is possible. It does not say which state my coatings or the commercial finish are in; that was not measured.

The equations behind it.

  • Wenzel, 1936 cosΘ* = r·cosθ

    The drop sinks into the texture. Roughness multiplies whatever the flat surface already does, so it makes a repellent surface more repellent and a wetting surface wetter.

  • Cassie and Baxter, 1944 cosΘ* = fs(cosθ + 1) − 1

    The drop bridges the texture and rests partly on trapped air, which has the lower energy when cosθ < (fs − 1)/(r − fs). Real drops do not always reach the lower energy state. This model only shows that one.

  • Furmidge, 1962 ρVg·sinα = γw(cosθrec − cosθadv)

    Roll off is a force balance: gravity down the slope against the grip of the drop's edge, set by the gap between its receding and advancing angles. That gap has to be measured on a real surface. Here it is a simple stand in, so treat the roll off number as an illustration of the trend, not a calculation.

This is a regular array of square pillars, not a woven fabric. It ignores water soaking into fibres, the two scales of roughness in a weave (fibres and yarns), and gravity flattening the drop.
